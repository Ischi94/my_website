## How does environmental history shape biodiversity?

Environmental change acts on ecosystems with a history. I investigate how past conditions influence ecological responses, and how we can distinguish those responses from the uneven record through which we observe them. My research links evidence across space and deep time with questions about biodiversity change today.

## Deep-time biodiversity

The fossil record offers observations of extinction and ecological reorganisation that cannot be replicated in modern ecosystems. I use these records to study climate legacies, origination and extinction, and the changing composition of marine communities.

My first-author studies examine [interacting climate changes and extinction risk](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-020-01377-w), [climate legacies and origination](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2105769118), and [spatial variation in foraminiferal assemblage responses](https://doi.org/10.1111/geb.13905).

## Climate change and conservation

Connecting past and present helps test ideas about vulnerability and ecological change. I work on how climate history, geographic ranges and environmental conditions relate to biodiversity responses, including a [systematic review of climate legacies and extinction](https://doi.org/10.1017/ext.2025.2) and collaborative research on [range change and extinction risk](https://doi.org/10.1111/geb.70226).

## Quantitative methods and causal inference

Observed patterns reflect both ecological processes and the way data are collected. I develop and apply statistical models to address systematic biases in ecological and fossil data, with particular interests in Bayesian modelling and causal inference. Collaborative work on [shark and ray diversity](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2025.12.017) illustrates the importance of accounting for uneven sampling when reconstructing biodiversity.

## Physical geography and environmental monitoring

Contemporary environmental data connect biodiversity research with human–environment questions. My collaborative work includes [snow-cover change in ski areas](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0299735), [camera-trap monitoring of visitors and wildlife](https://doi.org/10.1002/rse2.367), and [climate, plant–soil feedbacks and biological invasions](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.70110).

## Research direction

My aim is to connect these strands in analyses that explicitly address environmental history, spatial variation and observation bias. This means testing ecological explanations against independent evidence and making uncertainty part of the scientific result. I welcome collaborations that bring together fossil records, modern biodiversity observations and quantitative methods.
